# Drift Fever: content cluster brief

Written 10 August 2026. Self-contained. A writer working from this needs no prior context.

**You are writing seven pages. You are not writing the game, the embed, or the design.**

---

## 1. What this is

Drift Fever is an original browser drift racing game, live at **driftfever.com**. One
self-contained HTML file, no downloads, no accounts, no ads, no tracking, playable instantly
on a phone or a school Chromebook.

It has: a daily track split into three levels that everyone in the world drives the same day,
a ghost of your last run, tilt steering as an option, four original music tracks, and unlockable
cars.

Seven pages are being built on driftfever.com to bring people to it from search. You are writing
those pages.

---

## 2. The audience, which is not who you think

**School children, roughly 11 to 14, on managed Chromebooks, searching from school.**

This is not a guess. It is what the word "unblocked" means in these queries, and the seasonality
data confirms it: search volume for these terms collapses and rebuilds by a factor of roughly
fifty on the United States school calendar. June actuals were 6,600 for "car games unblocked"
against an annual average of 49,500.

Consequences for the writing:

- **Short sentences. Plain words.** No marketing voice, no hype, no "immerse yourself".
- **Say the useful thing first.** They are on a break and they want to play something.
- **Never condescend.** They will spot it instantly.
- **Never mention circumventing school filters, blocking, bypassing, or getting round anything.**
  Not once, in any wording. Describe games that happen to work in a browser with nothing to
  install. That is all.

---

## 3. Two rules that are project law, and are not negotiable

These come from the project brief and they govern every page.

**RULE 1: THE PAGE NEVER CLAIMS TO BE THE GAME THE SEARCHER TYPED.**

Somebody searching "drift boss unblocked" wants Drift Boss. Your page is not Drift Boss. Say so
plainly and early, in the first paragraph, and link out to the real game where one exists.

This is not just ethics, it is what makes the page defensible and what stops it reading as a
scam to a thirteen year old who has met a hundred of them.

**RULE 2: A NAMED GAME APPEARS ONLY REFERENTIALLY, IN THE TITLE AND H1, DESCRIBING WHAT THE
READER SEARCHED FOR AND NEVER WHAT IS ON THE PAGE.**

Acceptable: "Drift Boss unblocked: where to play it, and a drift game you can play here"
Not acceptable: "Play Drift Boss unblocked here", "Drift Boss 2", "the best Drift Boss clone"

Never imply endorsement, affiliation, or that we made it. Drift Boss belongs to Coolmath Games.
Drift Hunters belongs to its own publisher. Neither has anything to do with us.

## 5. What actually goes on a page

The long-tail data says these searchers are looking for **other specific games**: "driving mad
unblocked" at 27,100, "eggy car unblocked" at 18,100, "hill climb racing unblocked" at 2,400.

So the substance of a category page is **an honest, useful roundup of browser games of that
kind**, with Drift Fever as one of them and the only one playable on the page itself.

That is not a compromise. It is the thing that makes the page worth ranking, and it satisfies
Rule 1 by construction.

### Structure for a category page (1, 3, 5, 6, 7)

1. **H1** carrying the target phrase naturally.
2. **First paragraph, and it does the heavy lifting.** What this page is: a list of browser
   driving games that need no download, plus one you can play right here. Somebody who reads
   only this paragraph should know exactly what they are getting.
3. **The playable game.** Drift Fever, embedded. Placed high, because that is what they came for.
   A short, honest description: what it is, that it is free, that there is a new track every day
   and everyone drives the same one.
4. **The roundup.** Six to ten other games of the same kind. For each: what it is, what makes it
   good or bad, where to actually play it, linked. **Be honest, including about ours.**
5. **Something genuinely useful** that fits the page. Which games work on a Chromebook, which need
   a keyboard versus a touchscreen, which work offline once loaded.
6. **A short close.** No hard sell.

### Structure for a named-game page (2 and 4)

1. **H1** naming the game referentially, per Rule 2.
2. **First paragraph: say immediately that this is not that game, and link to where it actually
   is.** Do not bury this. Do not soften it.
3. **A genuinely useful section about the game they searched for**: what it is, who made it,
   where to play it legitimately, why it might be hard to reach.
4. **Then, and clearly separated: a drift game you can play here.** Drift Fever, embedded, with
   an honest account of how it differs from what they searched for.
5. **Other drift games worth playing**, linked out.

---

## 6. Voice

- Direct, plain, a bit dry. Never breathless.
- Australian or British spelling is fine; be consistent.
- No em dashes or en dashes. Restructure the sentence instead.
- No exclamation marks.
- Short paragraphs. This is read on a phone.
- **No keyword stuffing.** The target phrase belongs in the title, the H1, and once or twice
  naturally in the body. Anything more reads as spam to a reader and adds nothing for Google.

---

## 7. Technical requirements

- **Titles under about 60 characters**, target phrase near the front, and each one distinct.
- **Meta description around 150 characters**, written to be clicked rather than to repeat the title.
- **One H1 per page.** H2s for sections.
- **THE WORD "UNBLOCKED" GOES IN THE TITLE, HEADINGS AND BODY, AND STAYS OUT OF THE URL PATH.**
  Content filters keyword-match URLs, and the audience is behind those filters. URL keywords are a
  weak ranking factor while titles and headings are strong ones, so the word costs nothing where it
  is not and earns where it is.
  Good: `/car-games-online` · Bad: `/car-games-unblocked`
- **Every outbound link to another game is real, checked, and goes to a legitimate host** —
  the developer, or a major portal. Never to a clone site.
- Internal links between the seven pages where genuinely relevant. Not a link block on every page.

---

## 8. The embed, which is not your job

Each page carries Drift Fever playing inside an arcade cabinet frame. That component is being
built separately and you will be given it.

**Leave a clearly marked placeholder where it goes on each page.** Do not write the HTML for it,
do not size it, do not style it. Write the copy that sits above and below it.

For your information only, so the copy makes sense: the cabinet is a neon arcade machine with a
phone inside it, the game runs in the phone's screen, and there is a link to play it full size.

---

## 9. What to hand back

For each of the seven pages:

- Title tag and meta description
- URL slug, per the rule in section 7
- The full page copy, in markdown, with headings marked
- A marked placeholder for the game embed
- Every outbound link, with the URL you actually checked

Plus one short note covering: which games you recommended on each page and why they differ
between pages, anything you found while researching that contradicts this brief, and any page
you think will not rank and why.

**If something here is wrong, say so rather than working around it.** Disagreement has been right
more than once on this project.
